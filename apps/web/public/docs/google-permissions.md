## What ZeroDrive asks for

ZeroDrive asks Google for your verified email address, permission to manage the Drive files you choose to use with ZeroDrive, and access to a private application-data area. It does not ask for permission to browse or change every file already in your Google Drive.

The email address signs you into the correct ZeroDrive account and lets another ZeroDrive user find your public sharing identity. Personal files use Google Drive's limited `drive.file` permission. The encrypted file list and sharing-key backup use Google's hidden `appDataFolder` area.

## Why Google may email you

Google may send an account-access email after you approve these permissions. That message is generated by Google, not ZeroDrive. It summarizes the account information and Drive access you approved.

The email may sound broad because Google describes what an app is technically allowed to do. In ZeroDrive's case, the app uses those permissions for encrypted files created or selected through ZeroDrive and its own hidden configuration data.

## What happens if permission is missing

Signing in and granting Drive access are related but separate decisions on Google's consent screen. If you sign in without approving the required Drive permissions, ZeroDrive can recognize your account but cannot load or save your encrypted Storage files.

The app will show a Drive-access explanation before allowing Storage and sharing actions that require Google Drive. You can sign out and reconnect, then approve the requested Drive permissions.

## What Google Drive receives

Before a personal file is uploaded, ZeroDrive encrypts its content and protected details in your browser. Google Drive receives an opaque `.zd` object rather than the original readable file.

Google still operates the storage account and can see normal operational information such as object size, timestamps, and that ZeroDrive used the Drive API. Client-side encryption is designed to keep the original content and protected filename unreadable to storage alone.
